\section*{Limitations}

Our experiments cover English histories whose answers depend on images. On text-dominant benchmarks such as Mem-Gallery, where raw retrieval is already strong, an earlier version of \vgs{} gave no gain over RAG@40, and we did not re-run the final method there. MemEye has eight scenario histories, and its lightweight-reader gain is not significant on its own; it varies by 2.5 points across selection draws and is sensitive to the reader instruction (Appendix~\ref{app:robust}). MemLens uses gold rounds derived from session annotations, which can omit useful context. VisualMem, where the strong-reader gain is largest, uses multiple-choice questions about generated images; CamRoll uses real photos, and there the strong reader gains nothing over RAG@40.

With a strong reader, \vgs{} improves on retrieval only when histories are long: on MemEye, MemLens, and CamRoll it matches RAG@40 and a reranker over the 100 most similar rounds. Its gains also depend on the selector. GPT-5-mini recovers evidence sets on MemEye almost as completely as our selector and reproduces the MemLens gain, but it loses to RAG@40 on the 44k-token VisualMem index, and two open-weight selectors gave no gain on any benchmark (Appendix~\ref{app:robust}). The readers, the captioner, and one judge are Gemini models. Reading the whole index costs more as the history grows: at about 4,000 rounds \vgs{} costs four times as much as RAG@40 with the strong reader. Assistant replies are not indexed, so evidence stated only by the assistant must be found through the user's turns. The gate measures lexical visibility at the level of a history; it does not identify which questions benefit from widening. The memory-system baselines run with released code and our shared models, and cost comparisons depend on one provider's prices and caching terms.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

\vgs{} stores raw conversations and images, image descriptions, index lines, notes, and an index cache at the model provider. The same retention and deletion rules must apply to all of them. Deleting a round must also remove records derived from it and invalidate the cached index. Deployment requires per-user isolation, access control, and consent to storage by the provider. Incomplete evidence sets can produce incorrect counts, and \vgs{} does not detect missing members. All experiments use public research benchmarks and involve no new data from human participants. CamRoll contains real photos, some of them of people, that were published under Creative Commons licences; we use it under its research licence and redistribute no images.
