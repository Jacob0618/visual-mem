\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Benchmarks.} We evaluate on three benchmarks whose answers depend on images. MemEye~\citep{guo2026memeye} contains 371 questions over eight scenario histories of 126.5 rounds on average. MemLens~\citep{ren2026memlens} gives every question its own history of 64.6 rounds (32k) or 124.8 rounds (64k), and its gold rounds are the answer-session rounds with an evidence turn or image; we report its test split of 466 questions, and the threshold $\tau$ and the prompts were set on MemEye and the remaining MemLens questions. VisualMem~\citep{nguyen2026personalvisual} has ten personas with histories of 1,016.6 rounds and 172 images each, and 696 multiple-choice questions about facts that only the images show; it primarily evaluates locating a single answer-bearing image within a long history. We score the option letter and downscale images to 1,024 pixels.

\paragraph{Models.} The lightweight reader is \texttt{gemini-2.5-flash-lite} (temperature 0, no thinking), and the strong reader, the selector, and the captioner are \texttt{gemini-3.8-flash} (low thinking). The note writer is the lightweight model, and \texttt{gemini-embedding-2} embeds each round as its text plus its image descriptions.

\paragraph{Baselines and controls.} RAG@$k$ gives the reader the $k$ rounds most similar to the question, in chronological order and with the original images; $k{=}40$ is the strongest budget for both readers. Full-context reading gives the reader the whole history. Three same-model controls see the same candidates as \qss{}: a ranking by usefulness and a ranking with the \qss{} instruction, both cut at the size that \qss{} chose, and graded relevance (0--3) that keeps grades of 2 or higher.

\paragraph{Memory systems.} As references, we compare with six recent memory systems. Four write text at ingestion: SimpleMem~\citep{liu2026simplemem} compresses dialogue into facts, Omni-SimpleMem~\citep{liu2026omnisimplemem} extends it to images with a knowledge graph, MIRIX~\citep{wang2025mirix} writes typed memories with six agents, and TeleMem~\citep{chen2026telemem} extracts Mem0-style memories. V-Mem~\citep{kang2026vmem} and MMA~\citep{lu2026mma} keep raw turns and route retrieval by modality or weight it by confidence. For a controlled comparison, every system builds its memory from the same rounds and image descriptions with the same Gemini models, and its retrieved memory goes to the same readers, prompts, and judges (Appendix~\ref{app:baselines}); on MemLens they use 150 test questions stratified by type.

\paragraph{Metrics and statistics.} Retrieval is scored by full-set recall (Eq.~\ref{eq:fsr}) and the number of opened rounds. Accuracy uses the official judge of each benchmark, namely the 0--1 rubric of MemEye with a Gemini judge and the binary MemLens judge with Qwen3-VL-235B; a second judge family preserves every ordering (Appendix~\ref{app:robust}). Cost is reported per 1,000 questions at list prices from measured tokens; Appendix~\ref{app:latency} adds token F1, BLEU-1, Recall@10, nDCG@10, and latency. Differences are paired over questions with 95\% bootstrap intervals.
